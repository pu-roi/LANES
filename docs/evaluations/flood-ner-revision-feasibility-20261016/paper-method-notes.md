# Feasibility analysis method and limits

The existing 400-record AI silver dataset was not revised. This analysis fixes 332 unaffected records and their exact splits, preserving the complete 60-record test partition and ten training pilots. It tests filling 18 English/36 Taglish training and 12 English/two Taglish validation slots using saved sources under the unchanged six-label coverage policy, publisher/article/group limits and historical window.

The existing grouped MILP was reused inside an ignored analysis helper. Additional constraints pin fixed-record split indicators and prohibit overlapping/near-copy alternatives. Group overlays come from the hash-bound 342-source review; no physical event identity was invented. Solver status 2 is recorded as infeasible; timeout, a verified feasible incumbent, invalid evidence and optimality are distinct. Reruns on the same inputs reproduce all four infeasibility statuses.

The first shortening scenario deliberately includes location-positive paragraphs only. The supplemental scenario adds location-negative paragraphs rather than treating absence of entities as exclusion. All 106 proposals preserve whole source paragraph boundaries and require fresh language/complete-annotation/source-use review; inherited language and projected labels are hypotheses. The tested compact-paragraph heuristic does not exhaust all sentence or paragraph possibilities. No conditional or accepted 400-record witness was found, and no new partitions were frozen.

Source hashes, publication evidence, exact offsets and tokenizer alignment are checked with existing offline helpers and the saved lanes.xx.address-v3 tokenizer under spaCy 3.7.5. This tokenization is not trained-model inference. Mathematical quotas, structural annotation checks, substantive annotation review, source-use decisions and training admission remain separate outcomes.

The analysis identifies necessary relaxed upper-bound deficits, not a scientifically optimal dataset design or model accuracy. Existing private academic-use bases cover their recorded scope; unused passages require additional selected-extent assessment. RMN has no new use recommendation or admission. All twelve nonacademic outcomes and current source-use holds are preserved. No requirement for universal publisher correspondence or invented institutional approval is introduced.
